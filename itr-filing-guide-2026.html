Filing your Income Tax Return can feel intimidating the first time you sit down to do it — a maze of forms, deadlines and jargon like AIS, TDS and Section 87A. The good news is that for most salaried individuals, the entire process now takes less than 30 minutes once you have your documents ready. This Income Tax Return Filing Guide 2026 walks you through everything a beginner needs — from figuring out who must file, to choosing the right ITR form, entering income, claiming deductions, and finally e-verifying your return so your refund lands in your bank account without delay.

Whether you're a first-time filer fresh out of college, a salaried professional, a freelancer, or someone managing rental income, this guide on ITR filing for beginners is written in plain English so you can follow along and file confidently for Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27.

Quick Summary

An Income Tax Return is a self-declaration of your income and taxes to the government. Most salaried taxpayers should file using ITR-1 before the due date of 31 July 2026, using Form 16, Form 26AS and AIS as reference documents, and then complete e-verification within 30 days.

What is Income Tax Return (ITR)?

An Income Tax Return (ITR) is a prescribed form through which a taxpayer reports their total income earned during a financial year, the taxes already paid (via TDS or advance tax), and the final tax liability or refund due. It is filed with the Income Tax Department, typically online through the e-filing portal.

Think of your ITR as an annual report card of your finances. It captures income from salary, house property, capital gains, business or profession, and other sources such as interest and dividends, and reconciles it against the tax already deducted at source by your employer or bank. If you've paid more tax than you owed, filing your Income Tax Return India is how you claim that money back as a refund.

Why Filing ITR is Important

Many people assume ITR filing is only for those who "owe" tax, but that's far from the truth. Filing your return — even a nil return — has real, practical benefits:

💰

Claim Refunds

Get back excess TDS deducted by your employer or bank.

🏦

Loan Approvals

Banks ask for 2-3 years of ITRs for home, car or personal loans.

✈️

Visa Applications

Most embassies require ITR copies as proof of financial stability.

📄

Income Proof

Serves as legal proof of income for freelancers and self-employed people.

⚖️

Carry Forward Losses

Capital or business losses can only be carried forward if filed on time.

🛡️

Avoid Penalties

Filing on time avoids late fees, interest and scrutiny notices.

Who Should File an ITR

You are required to file an Income Tax Return for AY 2026-27 if any of the following apply to you:

  • Your gross total income before deductions exceeds the basic exemption limit under the tax regime you choose.
  • You have deposited more than ₹1 crore in one or more current accounts, or ₹50 lakh in savings accounts, during the year.
  • You have spent more than ₹2 lakh on foreign travel for yourself or another person.
  • Your electricity bill payments exceeded ₹1 lakh in the financial year.
  • You hold assets or have signing authority in accounts located outside India, or you are a beneficiary of a foreign asset.
  • You want to claim a refund of TDS deducted on salary, fixed deposits, or other income.
  • You have earned short-term or long-term capital gains from selling shares, mutual funds or property.
  • You are a company, partnership firm, or LLP, regardless of profit or loss.
  • You wish to carry forward business or capital losses to future years.

Who is Exempt from Filing ITR

You may not be required to file a return if:

  • Your total income is below the basic exemption limit and none of the mandatory conditions listed above apply to you.
  • You are a resident senior citizen (75 years or above) with only pension and interest income from the same bank where you've submitted the required declaration, and that bank has already deducted applicable tax.
  • Your only income is agricultural income and it is fully exempt under the Income Tax Act.
Tip

Even if you are technically exempt, filing a "nil" return is a good habit — it builds a clean financial history that helps with future loans, visas and credit applications.

Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime

Choosing between the old and new regime is one of the most important decisions while filing your Income Tax Return. The old regime allows a long list of deductions and exemptions — such as Section 80C, HRA and home loan interest — but taxes income at higher rates. The new regime offers lower slab rates but removes most deductions except a few like the standard deduction and employer's NPS contribution.

Important

If you claim significant deductions such as HRA, home loan interest and 80C investments, calculate your tax under both regimes before deciding — don't assume the new regime is always cheaper. Check with our HRA Calculator and Income Tax Calculator to compare.

Consider a simple example: Priya earns a gross salary of ₹12 lakh a year. Under the new regime, after the ₹75,000 standard deduction, her taxable income works out to roughly ₹11.25 lakh, and thanks to the slab structure and rebate rules, her effective tax burden is quite low. Under the old regime, if Priya pays ₹1.5 lakh towards 80C investments, ₹25,000 towards health insurance under 80D, and claims ₹1.8 lakh in HRA exemption, her taxable income could drop closer to ₹8.2 lakh — which may or may not beat the new regime once you apply the actual slab rates. This is exactly why a side-by-side calculation matters more than following a general rule of thumb; someone with heavy deductions may still come out ahead in the old regime, while someone with few investments almost always benefits from the new regime's lower rates.

PAN and Aadhaar Requirements

Your PAN (Permanent Account Number) is your primary identifier on the income tax portal, and it must be linked with your Aadhaar number. An unlinked or inoperative PAN can prevent you from logging in, e-verifying your return, or receiving your refund. If you haven't linked them yet, do so on the official income tax e-filing portal before you begin filing — this is one of the most common reasons beginners get stuck midway through the process.

Understanding AIS and Form 26AS

Form 26AS is a consolidated tax statement that shows the TDS deducted on your income, tax collected at source, advance tax and self-assessment tax paid against your PAN. AIS (Annual Information Statement) goes a step further — it compiles a much wider set of financial information reported by banks, employers, mutual funds and registrars, including salary, interest, dividends, mutual fund transactions, and even high-value purchases.

Before filing, always cross-check your income figures against both AIS and Form 26AS. If you spot a mismatch — say, interest income reported by your bank that you forgot to include — you can raise feedback directly on the AIS portal or adjust your return accordingly to avoid a future notice.

Different ITR Forms Explained

The Income Tax Department prescribes different forms based on the nature and amount of your income. Picking the wrong form is a common beginner mistake, so use this quick reference:

FormWho Should Use It
ITR-1 (Sahaj)Resident individuals with salary, one house property, and other sources income up to ₹50 lakh (no capital gains).
ITR-2Individuals/HUFs with capital gains, multiple house properties, or foreign income/assets, but no business income.
ITR-3Individuals/HUFs having income from business or profession, including freelancers and consultants.
ITR-4 (Sugam)Individuals, HUFs and firms (other than LLP) opting for the presumptive taxation scheme with turnover within prescribed limits.
Beginner Tip

Most salaried employees with no capital gains, no foreign assets, and total income under ₹50 lakh should simply use ITR-1 — it is the shortest and simplest form.

Step-by-Step Online Filing Process

Here's the complete process of how to file ITR online, broken into clear, manageable steps:

  1. Log in to the e-filing portal

    Visit the official Income Tax e-filing website and log in using your PAN as the user ID, along with your password or Aadhaar OTP.

  2. Choose the Correct Assessment Year

    For income earned between April 2025 and March 2026, select Assessment Year 2026-27 — this is a step beginners often get wrong by confusing FY with AY.

  3. Select the Right ITR Form

    Use the reference table above, or let the portal's "Help me decide" tool suggest the correct form based on your income sources.

  4. Choose Your Filing Mode

    Most taxpayers file online directly on the portal, which pre-fills salary, TDS and AIS data automatically to save time.

  5. Enter and Verify Income Details

    Review pre-filled salary, interest, and other income data against your Form 16 and bank statements, correcting any discrepancies.

  6. Claim Applicable Deductions

    If filing under the old regime, enter your Section 80C, 80D, HRA and home loan interest details accurately with proof on hand.

  7. Verify Your Tax Liability

    The portal automatically computes your final tax payable or refund due after adjusting TDS and advance tax already paid.

  8. Pay Any Self-Assessment Tax Due

    If additional tax is payable, pay it online via the portal before submitting your return, and note the challan details.

  9. Submit and E-Verify

    Submit your return, then e-verify it immediately using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or a Demat/bank EVC — an unverified return is treated as not filed.

If the portal shows an outstanding tax liability after adjusting your TDS, you'll need to pay this as self-assessment tax before you can submit the return. This is common for people with additional income sources such as freelance work, interest on fixed deposits, or capital gains, where TDS deducted during the year doesn't fully cover the final liability. The portal generates a challan you can pay directly through net banking, and the payment is reflected in your Form 26AS almost immediately, letting you proceed to submission without delay. If you routinely have a large shortfall each year, it's worth exploring advance tax payments in quarterly instalments during the financial year itself, so you avoid both a large one-time outflow and any interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

E-Verification Methods

Once submitted, your return must be e-verified within 30 days. You can e-verify instantly using:

  • Aadhaar OTP sent to your registered mobile number.
  • Net banking login through your bank's e-filing linkage.
  • Bank account or Demat account EVC (Electronic Verification Code).
  • Sending a signed physical ITR-V form by post to the CPC Bengaluru, if you're unable to verify online.

Checking Your ITR Status

After e-verification, you can track your return's progress — "Submitted," "Under Processing," "Processed," or "Refund Issued" — under the "e-File" tab on the portal. It's a good habit to check status weekly until your return is fully processed.

Income Tax Refund Process

If you've paid more tax than your actual liability — usually via excess TDS — the difference is refunded directly to your pre-validated bank account. Make sure your bank account is validated and linked with your PAN on the e-filing portal, since refunds are only issued electronically.

Return Filed & E-VerifiedProcessing usually begins within a few days.

Refund ProcessedTypically issued within 4-6 weeks of successful e-verification.

Common Mistakes Beginners Make

  • Selecting the wrong Assessment Year or ITR form for their income type.
  • Forgetting to report interest income from savings accounts and fixed deposits.
  • Not reconciling income with AIS and Form 26AS before submission.
  • Skipping e-verification, which makes the entire filing invalid.
  • Choosing a tax regime without comparing actual numbers first.
  • Entering an unvalidated or closed bank account for refund credit.
  • Missing the due date and paying avoidable late fees and interest.

Most of these mistakes stem from rushing through the process in the last week of July when the portal is heavily loaded and support queries take longer to resolve. Beginners in particular tend to skip cross-checking their AIS, assuming that whatever the employer reported in Form 16 is the complete picture — but banks, mutual funds and even your side-gig clients may report income directly to the tax department that never shows up on your Form 16. Building a habit of downloading and reviewing your AIS in June, well before the deadline, resolves the vast majority of these issues calmly and without penalty.

Benefits of Filing ITR

Why It's Worth the Effort

Beyond refunds, timely ITR filing builds a strong financial track record that simplifies future loan approvals, visa applications, credit card upgrades, and even government tenders. It also protects you from scrutiny notices that often arise from unexplained high-value transactions reported in AIS.

Penalties for Late Filing

Missing the due date doesn't mean you can't file at all — you can still submit a belated return until 31 December of the assessment year — but it comes at a cost:

Late Filing Fee (Section 234F)

Up to ₹5,000 if filed after the due date, reduced to ₹1,000 for taxpayers with total income up to ₹5 lakh. Interest under Section 234A also applies on any unpaid tax from the original due date.

Final Conclusion

Filing your Income Tax Return doesn't need to be stressful. Once you understand which category you fall into, gather your PAN, Aadhaar, Form 16, Form 26AS and AIS, and pick the correct ITR form, the rest of the process is largely guided by the e-filing portal itself. The most important habits to build are: reconciling your income against AIS every year, choosing the tax regime that actually saves you the most money rather than the default one, and e-verifying your return the moment you submit it.
